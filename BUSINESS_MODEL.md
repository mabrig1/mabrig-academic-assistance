# Mabrig Academic Assistance — UNN Student Workforce Business Model

## 1. Model
Mabrig operates a managed campus academic/document-services marketplace. Customers submit legitimate academic-support, formatting, printing, binding, data-support and delivery orders. The platform prices and tracks the order; trained part-time student workers complete approved operational tasks; a supervisor/QA layer checks work before delivery.

## 2. Customer segments
- UNN undergraduate and postgraduate students
- Researchers and lecturers
- Departments, student associations and campus organisations
- Nearby SMEs and professionals needing document services

## 3. Value proposition
### Customers
One intake point for document preparation, formatting, printing, binding, research support, data assistance and campus delivery with tracking and quality control.
### Student workers
Flexible paid work, practical digital/business experience, verified performance history and progression into specialist or team-lead roles.

## 4. Revenue engines
1. Document formatting and production fees
2. Printing and binding margin
3. Editing/proofreading/research-support fees
4. Data-analysis support fees
5. Campus delivery/service fees
6. Express turnaround surcharge
7. Institutional/bulk orders
8. Optional membership/priority-service plans

## 5. Order economics
For each order record: customer price; direct production cost; worker compensation; delivery cost; payment fee; refund/rework reserve; platform contribution margin.

Do not use a hidden percentage-only worker model. Show the worker's task rate before acceptance. Set service prices high enough to fund fair compensation, production, QA, payment charges, rework and platform margin.

Suggested internal target allocation for planning only (not a wage rule):
- 25–40% fulfilment labour depending on task intensity
- 10–30% physical production/materials where applicable
- 5–15% delivery/payment/rework overhead
- remaining contribution margin funds supervision, software, marketing, taxes/compliance and profit

Management should replace these percentages with actual unit economics after the first 100 paid orders.

## 6. Workforce structure
- Applicant
- Trainee/probation worker
- Verified Worker — Level 1
- Specialist — Level 2
- Senior Specialist / QA — Level 3
- Team Lead / Operations Coordinator

Performance score: quality 40%, on-time completion 25%, customer outcome 15%, communication 10%, compliance 10%. Do not use protected personal characteristics in scoring.

## 7. Recruitment standard
1. Online application
2. Identity and student-status verification
3. Skills/availability screening
4. Role-specific practical test using dummy data/documents
5. Structured interview with common scoring rubric
6. Reference/guarantor check only where proportionate to the role
7. Confidentiality, privacy, academic-integrity and customer-service orientation
8. Probationary assignments under QA
9. Activation into worker pool

Never charge applicants recruitment fees. Maintain written engagement terms appropriate to the actual employment/contract relationship and obtain Nigerian legal/accounting advice before scaling.

## 8. Assignment engine
Each order becomes one or more work tickets with required skill, SLA, task rate, deadline, data-access level and QA checklist. Matching ranks available verified workers by skill, current workload, reliability and declared availability. Workers may accept or decline without losing unrelated opportunities.

## 9. Quality control
- File/version audit trail
- Standard checklists per service
- Mandatory QA for new workers
- Random QA for established workers
- Rework reason codes
- Customer acceptance/complaint flow
- Escalation to supervisor
- Worker access removed automatically after ticket closure where possible

## 10. Academic integrity
Allowed: tutoring, proofreading, editing, formatting, reference organization, research assistance, data-processing guidance and document production. Do not market impersonation, fabricated research/data, exam assistance or submission of ghostwritten assessed work as a student's own work.

## 11. Admin dashboard requirements
- Recruitment funnel and application status
- Worker profiles, skills, availability and verification
- Practical-test/interview scores
- Assignment queue and matching
- Task acceptance/rejection
- Time/SLA tracking
- QA and rework
- Earnings ledger and payout status
- Referral attribution
- Complaints/incidents
- Role-based customer-file permissions
- Business KPIs: orders, GMV, average order value, fulfilment cost, contribution margin, turnaround, rework rate, repeat customers and worker utilization

## 12. Compliance checkpoint
Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment distinguishes ordinary hiring from operating as a Private Employment Agency/labour contractor. If Mabrig later employs people for placement with third-party client companies, management should determine whether a Recruiter's Licence is required before offering that service. Keep the initial model focused on workers fulfilling Mabrig's own customer orders unless compliance advice supports expansion.

## 13. 90-day pilot
### Phase 1 — first 2 weeks
Recruit 10–15 workers across formatting, production, data support, customer support and delivery. Use practical tests and supervised trial tasks.
### Phase 2 — weeks 3–6
Route real paid orders through tickets. Measure margin, turnaround, QA failure, rework and worker reliability weekly.
### Phase 3 — weeks 7–12
Retain the strongest worker pool, introduce levels/bonuses, recruit for skill gaps, approach departments/associations for bulk work, and update prices from measured unit economics.

## 14. North-star metric
Paid orders delivered correctly and on time at positive contribution margin, while giving student workers transparent, reliable compensation and useful work experience.
